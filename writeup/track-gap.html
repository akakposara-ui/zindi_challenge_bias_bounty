The Track Gap

On Arizona's reservations, many homes are reached only by roads that Overture classes as track. The challenge's escape-route road layer leaves that class out and the automated road metric never looks below named highways, so these homes drop off the network in both. Part B documents the label-free model behind our leaderboard submissions.

Part A
Best Bias Discovery

Homes reached only by roads the escape-route layer drops

A1. The pattern

The challenge ships a scored road layer, <region>-overture-roads.parquet. It keeps the drivable classes motorway, trunk, primary, secondary, tertiary, residential and unclassified ("escape-route framing"). The unfiltered extract of the same Overture release shows what that filter drops: mostly track and service.

Overture inherits these classes from OpenStreetMap. There, a dirt road is often tagged track whatever it serves. On the Navajo Nation and the other Arizona reservations in the package, those dirt roads are the public roads that reach people's homes. For a deterministic 10 % sample of Overture building footprints, we measured the distance to the nearest road of each class group. Many structures on reservations have a track within 100 m and no road of the escape-route layer at all. Off-reservation tracts of the same rurality rarely look like that.

A2. Why the automated scorecard cannot see it

The scorecard's road metric compares Overture motorway to secondary against TIGER S1100 and S1200, so it only covers named highways. On these reservations the highways are there. Overture's named-class length is 1.0 to 2.0 times its route-numbered highway length, the network TIGER scores against, so the metric reports little or no road gap. The access roads sit below the highway classes, outside the metric. Classed track, they are also outside the escape-route layer.

The pattern is therefore invisible in all five scorecard metrics and in every predefined stratum. Tribal against non-tribal road equity looks fine, because the measured roads are fine.

A4. Who is affected and what it means

About 4,300 structures on Arizona reservation tracts are reached only by tracks, by the sample estimate. Most are on the Navajo Nation (about 2,700), the Tohono O'odham Nation (about 800), San Carlos and Gila River. These tracts have some of the highest social vulnerability in the package: median SVI 0.87 to 0.94. San Carlos and Fort Apache rank in the top 3 % of their region for wildfire hazard potential, and Gila River and Salt River in the top 7 %.

The roads in question are public. The Navajo Nation Department of Transportation reports that the BIA and the Navajo Nation own 76 % of the Nation's 14,167 miles of road, and that 85 % of the 10,884 miles they maintain are dirt.

  • Evacuation routing. A route built on the escape-route layer, or on any routing profile that drops track, has no first segment for these homes. The nearest drivable road can be hundreds of metres to kilometres away, and the route ignores the road residents actually use. For San Carlos and Fort Apache, the reservations with the highest wildfire hazard, that is the road out during a fire.
  • Emergency dispatch. Dispatch and travel-time tools that snap an address to the nearest drivable road snap these homes to the wrong road. They under-estimate the last leg on a dirt road that rain or snow can close.
  • Relief logistics. Water hauling, food and medical deliveries plan stops on the same graphs. Homes beyond the drivable network fall out of the plan, or show up as unreachable.
  • Measurement. Coverage audits that score named highways, this challenge's included, rate these communities as well mapped. That steers mapping effort and funding elsewhere.

What would fix it. In an escape-route layer, treat a track or service segment as drivable when it is the nearest road to a building, or when it carries a public route name. Add an access metric to the scorecard (the share of structures within 100 m of the scored network), stratified by tribal status and rurality. Upstream, tagging residential dirt roads as unclassified or residential with surface=unpaved keeps them in routing graphs.

A5. Reproduce it

Only challenge data is read: the Overture building and road extracts of release 2026-08-19.0 and the strata tables, straight from the public bucket. From the repository root:

python src/features.py && python src/roads.py      # per-tract features (also used by the model)
python analysis/road_access.py   # distances for the 10 % footprint sample; ~10 min for AZ, OK, CA and WA
python analysis/summarise.py     # writes analysis/out/tables.md: every table above

A6. Limits

  • Footprints are structures, not households. Outbuildings inflate the counts but not the shares within a tract much.
  • A 100 m threshold suits clustered homes; homes on long driveways can be flagged in any group. That is why we report 200 m too, and compare against tracts of the same rurality rather than in absolute terms.
  • Some track tags describe the surface truthfully. The bias is in the consequence: a class rule built for farm and forest roads removes residential access roads from drivable networks.
  • Reservation tracts are few (25 in Arizona), so per-reservation figures rest on hundreds of sampled structures. The four Navajo Nation tracts above each show the pattern on their own.
Part B
Best Documentation

A label-free model of the organisers' coverage-gap score

B1. Approach

No labels exist, and the reference layers the score was built from (TIGER roads, Microsoft footprints, USGS facilities, CBP establishments) were removed from the package. Rebuilding them from outside copies is prohibited. So the model treats each removed reference quantity as an unknown and draws it from a generative model conditioned on permitted features. It then applies the organisers' published formula to every draw, and submits the mean over 2,000 draws, which is the RMSE-optimal estimate under the model.

The formula: each component is 1 − min(1, overture / reference) where a reference exists. The POI component is the mean of its defined halves (USGS facilities; all places against CBP). The score is the mean of the defined components, so draws with no defined component are discarded: the organisers dropped those tracts.

Outside facts, all general or documented: the national CBP rate of about 0.024 establishments per resident; the organisers' README and challenge statement; and two schema details of the removed layers: the CBP columns (cbp_estab, cbp_estab_res, cbp_estab_bus) and the Microsoft columns (height, confidence), as described in a public repository (theerealhenry/Mapping-Equity-Coverage-Gaps). These are column names only: no value, code or data from that repository was used. Both hypotheses were then tested, on the leaderboard (v2a against v2b) or against the challenge data. No TIGER, Microsoft, HIFLD/USGS or CBP data was read.

B3. Components

  • Roads. TIGER S1100+S1200 length is proxied by Overture roads carrying an Interstate, US or state route number, and state subsystems such as Loop, Spur and Business. Each tract counts roads inside it plus numbered roads running along its boundary within 25 m, because TIGER roads that form a tract boundary count for both tracts. Texas FM/RM roads and Oklahoma county roads enter with probabilities 0.1 and 0.4 (see B4). Overture length is computed exactly as specified (motorway to secondary).
  • Buildings. The removed Microsoft layer had height, confidence: it is the Global ML release that Overture itself conflates (version 2025-02-25). So the reference equals the ML footprints Overture kept plus r × its OSM and Esri footprints. Here r, the ML footprints each OSM footprint replaced, is inferred from the strata USFS building count. In 678 ML-only tracts, that count tracks ML with a median log ratio of −0.007 and an sd of 0.146.
  • Facilities. For fire stations, EMS and schools:
    • the real count is drawn as Poisson;
    • Overture detects each facility with probability po, adds duplicates and false positives;
    • USGS records each facility with probability ph;
    • the real count is drawn from its exact posterior given the Overture count.
    The implied Overture/reference ratios are 0.84, 1.2 and 1.6, consistent with "EMS close to 1:1, fire stations carry the component".
  • Establishments. ZIP establishments are split over tracts by business-address share. They are proxied from Overture places and large footprints, with ZIPs rebuilt from place postcodes (97 % carry one).

Two single-factor tests settled open questions. v3d beat v2b: the concave business proxy had put CBP gaps in residential tracts with few places, where business addresses are few too. v3e lost to v2b: removing Oklahoma's county roads from TIGER hurt the score. That confirms, on the leaderboard, what the organisers' 0.71 Overture/TIGER ratio implied without any labels.

Considered and rejected:

  • roads without the boundary band, which contradicts the published shares;
  • all Texas FM roads as S1200, which gives 18 % against 28 %;
  • a physical fire-station prior, which implies 480 to 1,070 stations for Maricopa against about 300;
  • parameter averaging over plausible ranges, kept in the code but changing predictions by 0.004 RMS only.

B7. Reproducibility

The repository holds run_all.sh, requirements.txt (duckdb 1.5.6, numpy 2.5.4, pandas 3.0.6, pyarrow 26.0.0, scikit-learn 1.9.1, scipy 1.18.1) and a README that follows Zindi's documentation guide. A clean run from an empty directory and a fresh environment takes 18 minutes on a 20-thread laptop: features 264 s, roads 540 s, postcodes and sizes 217 s, model 17 s per version. It reproduces the features exactly. Every model component has its own seeded random stream (seed 2026).

python -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt
PYTHON=.venv/bin/python ./run_all.sh v2b      # submissions/v2b.csv in the sample-submission layout
